## Study-design hierarchy
Weight: meta-analysis/systematic review > randomized experiment (educational RCT) > quasi-experiment (with a comparison group) > correlational > case/qualitative study. For learning-intervention effectiveness claims, prefer designs with a control group.

## Effect size & sample
Report the effect size (Cohen's d, Hedges' g), not just significance. Beware small samples and the wrong unit of analysis (student vs. class vs. school — mind clustering/design effects). Results from a single class/school are not generalized without qualification.

## Context-transfer limits
Education findings are highly context-dependent (curriculum, culture, language, resource level). An intervention that worked in one system may not transfer to the Indonesian context. State context assumptions explicitly.

## Interpretation
Separate proxy outcomes (short-term test scores) from meaningful ones (retention, transfer, long-term results). Avoid causal claims from correlational designs; flag publication bias when only positive results are available.
